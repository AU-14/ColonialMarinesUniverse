Eight facts, one screen

The "SERVER INFO" title and its rule are gone — the screen is only ever showing this, so a heading saying so is a row spent on nothing. Players and round time join the other six as equals. Three takes on the same two rules: no cell fills, because eight filled boxes inside the screen box is the nested-box problem at its worst; and the repeated labels factored out, because GOVFOR SHIP / OPFOR SHIP / GOVFOR PLATOON / OPFOR PLATOON says SHIP twice and PLATOON twice when the two forces could be named once each. D is the mix of A and C that came out of comparing them, and is the one worth building.

D — Hybrid, and the one to build. A's head and foot with C's forces block between them. It turned out to be more than the sum: because C's rows right-align their values and A's head and foot already put their values on the right, every value in the panel now lands on one right margin — COLONY FALL, all four force names, and NOT STARTED share an edge. Neither parent does that on its own. It keeps the planet at full stencil size, which was A's whole argument, and inherits C's indifference to value length, which was C's — the lopsided case puts NONE on the right margin with a long leader and nothing else moves.
A — Briefing. Reads top-down as a deployment order: where you are, then who is facing whom, then the state of the room. The two forces are columns under their own name, with a colour bar instead of a coloured box, and the row labels sit in a right-aligned gutter so SHIP and PLATOON are said once. The planet gets stencil at full size because it is the one fact you actually read from across the desk.
B — Mirrored. The confrontation is the layout: the two forces read outward from a centre spine that carries the row labels, so GOVFOR and OPFOR are physically opposed rather than merely adjacent. Strongest character of the three. I expected it to be the fragile one and it is not — because the values align inward against the spine, the lopsided case (one side with no ship at all) just leaves a short word hugging the middle, which is fine. What it actually costs is a ragged outer edge: the block's silhouette changes with the data, where A's columns stay where they are whatever is in them.
C — Manifest. A printout: every fact on its own left-labelled line with leader dots to its value. Least designed, most authentic to the period, and by far the most robust — it does not care how long any value is, and adding a ninth field later costs one line. The forces become an indented group under a coloured heading, which is what removes the repetition here.
No motion anywhere. Round time and player count are the two values that actually change, and the temptation is to mark them as live with a blink or a pulse. Deliberately not done: continuous modulation on a screen someone sits in front of is the thing this UI has been avoiding everywhere else.
Try the other two value sets. "Longest realistic" swaps in the longest strings these fields carry; "Lopsided" is real data copied off a running server — no map picked yet, GOVFOR with no ship, OPFOR with no platoon. All three layouts survive both, which was not a given and is the reason the toggle is here rather than a note claiming they would. The differences that remain are aesthetic: A keeps its columns fixed whatever the data, B's outer edge goes ragged, C does not move at all.
