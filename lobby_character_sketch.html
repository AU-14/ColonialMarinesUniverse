CMU · lobby · the character page

Give it the same bones as the other page

The character page has no structure today — it is one row floating in a box, which is why it reads as empty however the parts are arranged. The server page works because it has a skeleton: one big fact with a tag, a rule, the body, a rule, a foot. Borrowing that exact skeleton does two things at once. The switch starts reading as one machine showing two pages rather than two unrelated screens, and the buttons get an obvious home — the foot, where the server page already puts players and round time. Sprite stays at 3×. The three options differ only in the middle band.

Why the buttons belong on the page after all. There is a rule hiding in what we already built: the keys on the case are terminal commands — AHelp, Options, Leave — available whatever the screen shows. Customize and Ignore Allegiance belong to the character, which is one of two things the screen happens to be showing. Putting them on the case broke that rule, which is why they looked wrong there and why Customize made no sense on the server page. In the page's foot they are exactly as scoped as the thing they act on.
They still have to read as pressable, so the foot is cells told apart by fill and a hairline of void — the command band's own idiom. A fill says pressable without a frame, which keeps them clear of the nested-box trap the old round-info cells fell into.
A — Portrait. Sprite and role together on the left, arrows either side of the sprite. Closest to today, just given a head and a foot. Safest and least interesting.
B — Centred. The sprite is the page's subject, so it sits in the middle with the role beneath. Reads most like a dossier photo, and it is the only one where the sprite is unambiguously the point rather than an illustration beside some text.
C — Filed. Sprite left, role and modes as leader rows on the server page's own dotted rhythm. The most consistent of the three — flip the switch and the two pages are visibly the same instrument — and the easiest to add a row to later without a redesign. It also splits "INS+DS / Platoon Commander" into the two facts it actually is.
The head is the real move. On the server page the big fact is the planet and the tag is the gamemode. Here the big fact is the name and the tag is the pronoun and age. Same position, same weight, same rule under it — that is what makes the two pages feel like one device rather than two things sharing a box.
Still open: whether the arrows stay as those small side buttons. They are the one part I have not rethought, because changing how you move between characters is a behaviour change rather than a layout one.
